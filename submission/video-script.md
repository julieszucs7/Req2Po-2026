## Q2 — Walk through your project (94 seconds, ~220 words)

> This is Req2PO. The first phase is in production at my company now. We take three to five hundred invoices a week.
>
> In construction, the people placing orders aren't accountants. So invoices arrive with no PO number, the wrong job, or tax on a tax-exempt job.
>
> And an invoice that can't be entered isn't a late payment — it's an unrecorded liability. Job costs are understated, the close slips, and the CFO's numbers aren't complete.
>
> Our ERP is old and closed, so I built controls around it instead of replacing it. What you're seeing runs on made-up data; the real one runs on my employer's.

## Q3 — How AI reshapes your target role (84 seconds, ~195 words)

> I don't think AI replaces accountants. It moves where the judgement goes.
>
> Most of accounts payable isn't judgement. It's reading a document, finding the PO, checking the math, the tax, the duplicates. A model can read. Rules can check. So the mechanical errors go, and the data entry with them. What's left needs an accountant: deciding how something should be treated.
>
> So I was careful about what the AI decides, which is nothing. AI reads. Rules decide. A bot enters. A person reviews. Every check is a rule an auditor can read — "the model thought it was fine" is not an audit trail.
>
> *(cut if long: "Every check is a rule an auditor can read — the model thought it was fine is not an audit trail")*
>
> It also counts its own failures. Every problem gets a category. So I can tell you with a number, not a feeling, that about eighty percent of our invoices arrive with something to resolve — and which issues.
>
> That's not an accounts payable problem, it's a purchasing problem — and it shows up in the financial statements. Finding that is the controller's job, which is what I'm working toward.
>
> The work gets simpler to do and more demanding to think about — a training question as much as a software one.

> The opportunity nobody funds is small and mid-size companies on closed, old systems. The advice is to replace the ERP; they can't afford it, so they do nothing and keep paying in rework.
>
> *(cut if long: "The opportunity nobody funds is small and mid-size companies on closed, old systems. The advice is to replace the ERP; they can't afford it, so they do nothing and keep paying in rework")*
>
> You don't have to replace a system to put modern controls around it. The ERP stays the book of record, nobody retrains, and the error is caught before the entry, not corrected after.
>
> And it isn't really about construction. Every organization that buys things has the same problems; all that changes is what the cost codes to — a job for me, a client matter, a grant, a department. The checks are the same.
>
> That's what I want to keep building, and why this scholarship matters to me.

## Before you record

- Open the demo link in a private browsing window and confirm it loads.
- Process the inbox before you start, so you're not waiting on screen.
- Say the ERP is old and closed. **Don't name it, and don't name your employer.**
- Screen-share **only the demo**. No real invoices, vendors, screens or documents from work.
- You can say Phase 1 is in production, give the weekly volume and the eighty percent. Keep it at that level — the rules, prompts and screens of the real build belong to your employer.
- Say plainly that the demo runs on a mock system with fictional data. Honest scope is one of the things the council says it's looking for.
